  • 01 Look Under review · PVLDB · Co-first author

    Bridging the Modality Gap that Hurts Cross-Modal Retrieval

    Yongxi Yang*, Lining Yan*, Wenxin Zhou*, Taotao Zhan, Shaohui Yang, Guoxin Kang · *equal contribution, alphabetical

    Across modalities, the nearest vector is often not the relevant one. SPAR (Semantic Partitioning with Context-Aware Re-ranking) replaces geometric routing with a learned one: the corpus is split into capacity-balanced semantic buckets, a two-layer MLP router picks the top buckets in one forward pass, and the bounded candidate set makes three re-ranking corrections affordable — hubness correction, α-weighted query expansion, and a residual cross-encoder. New items are assigned to frozen centroids, so insertion needs no router retraining.

    • +7.5–42.5 pprouting recall over state-of-the-art baselines
    • +0.5–39.6 ppend-to-end recall over baselines
    • 7 / 7workloads above exhaustive cosine search in Recall@1

    Seven workloads on MS-COCO, VisualNews, NIGHTS, Fashion200K (M-BEIR), and Flickr30K.

  • 02 Carry In preparation · NAACL 2027

    GARDS-RAG: Globally Anchored Dependency-State Routing for Multi-Hop QA

    Multi-hop RAG fails in two ways: a subquestion loses the constraints of the original question (intent drift), or a step sees intermediate states it does not depend on (state contamination). GARDS-RAG keeps the original question as a global anchor and treats each DAG edge as a channel that passes only the direct predecessor’s subquestion, answer, and rationale into reranking and generation. This is a manuscript in preparation, not an accepted paper.

    • 40.8 vs 29.6EM on MuSiQue against the best baseline (DeepSeek-V4-Flash)
    • 67.6 vs 59.4EM on 2WikiMultiHopQA, same setting
    • 66%of 2-hop accuracy kept at 4 hops, vs 32–44% for baselines

    Same pattern across three LLMs (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-5.4 mini, Qwen 3.7 Flash) and two retrievers (BM25, Contriever).

  • 03 Ask Notre Dame CRC · July–August 2026 · Research intern

    Paper Atomizer: An Agentic Workflow for Scientific Evidence

    With Dr. Samuel Rund, Center for Research Computing, University of Notre Dame

    Paper Atomizer is the CRC’s tool for turning a research PDF into structured data — text, figures, tables, and chart values. My branch adds the agentic layer: a coordinator that turns a researcher’s goal (explore, extract data, figures, traits, or full review) into a processing plan, and an Ask system that answers questions from the paper with the evidence attached.

    Each answer is drafted, checked, and repaired before it is shown. The checks search Methods, Results, Discussion, and tables again before allowing “Not stated”; bind each number to its metric, life stage, and experiment, so a Figure 4 value cannot answer a question about Figure 3; and require every part of a multi-part question to be covered. I also moved the default OCR from a remote DeepSeek model to a local stack that handles scanned papers: PyMuPDF words, Tesseract, and PP-DocLayout for figure and table boxes.

    • 8evidence checks before an answer is shown
    • 5research goals mapped to a processing plan
    • 19klines added across 300 files on my branch

    FastAPI, React + TypeScript, PostgreSQL, Gemini, PyMuPDF, Tesseract, PP-DocLayout. Code: crcresearch/paper-atomizer, branch Cris/agentic-workflow (private repository).

  • Published · CSCI 2025 · First author

    Multi-Level Text Feature Analysis for Detecting AI-Generated Content

    Lining Yan, Haoru Ye, Tailin Pan, Baha Ihnaini · Wenzhou-Kean University

    Scientific abstracts from the CHEAT dataset, read at two levels: the whole abstract and each sentence. Human abstracts vary more in sentence length (10–50 words) and lexical diversity (TTR 0.5–0.7); AI abstracts are more uniform (15–30 words, TTR around 0.4). A ModernBERT + random forest ensemble uses both levels, and SHAP attributions concentrate on perplexity and token log-probability.

    • 99.9%F1 on abstracts
    • 93.5%F1 on sentences
    • 5models compared: LSTM, RF, BERT, ModernBERT, ensemble
  • In preparation · Senior capstone

    Pattern-Centric Detection of AI-Generated Text: An Interpretable and Data-Efficient Expert System

    Detectors usually need thousands of labels and explain nothing. Here, KMeans over statistical, syntactic, and semantic features builds a small Generative Pattern Library; an Explainable Boosting Machine is trained only on the 44–50 cluster representatives, and every prediction comes with a SHAP attribution. No white-box access to the generating model is needed.

    • 0.9945AUROC on HC3 with 44–50 labeled samples
    • >95%of full-model performance at ~1/100 of the labels
    • 5 / 5benchmarks above zero-shot DetectGPT

    Benchmarks: DAIGT, HC3, LLMDetect, MAGE, MultiModel. Top signals: inter-sentence coherence, type-token ratio, pronoun ratio.

  • Published · AgriEngineering 2025 · Second author

    MCP-X: An Ultra-Compact CNN for Rice Disease Classification in Resource-Constrained Environments

    Xiang Zhang, Lining Yan, Belal Abuhaija, Baha Ihnaini · AgriEngineering 7(11), 359

    A rice-disease classifier small enough for on-device use, trained from scratch with no pretraining. Expert routing diversifies feature branches, efficient channel attention reweights them, and a bi-level recurrent encoder–decoder captures lesion scale and texture. My contribution is writing—review and editing, and visualization.

    • 0.21 Mparameters, about 1 MB in memory
    • 98.93%accuracy on PlantVillage; 96.59% on rice field images
    • 16.7×fewer parameters than MobileNetV2
