I am an M.S. student in Computer Science at Yale, in the Inquisitive Robotics Lab with Professor Tesca Fitzgerald. I work on reliable and adaptive agents: systems that have to notice when the evidence for a step is missing.

Before Yale I worked on detection, on multimodal retrieval, and on an agent that reads scientific papers. A model can score a finished string, retrieve a region, or extract a field, and still not know what to do when that step is not enough. That decision is the research.

About

I am interested in agents that stay reliable when a step is underdetermined. In particular:

  1. When to look again. An agent should retrieve again, open another modality, or stop, instead of continuing on a thin match. (VLDB 2026, under review: the manuscript studies that choice as routing, not as another full scan of the corpus.)
  2. What a later step is allowed to see. The original question has to stay in force, and an early error should not become the next context. (GARDS-RAG, in preparation; a document agent built with Samuel Rund at Notre Dame in summer 2026.)
  3. When to ask a person. The question should name the missing piece, and the answer should change the next attempt, not only the current field. (Yale, since September 2026. This project is early. I am not reporting a result.)
